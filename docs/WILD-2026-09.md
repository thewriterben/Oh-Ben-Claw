# WILD → OBC: a closed-loop edge datalogger as the model for the spinal tier

Date: 2026-09-13. Companion to `CONNECTOME-2026-09.md` (the survey this
continues — §2.2 there is the seam this document builds on).
Status: **step 1 shipped and measured** (four commits, 7c6a92c..8ae861f);
steps 2–3 are a plan with their preconditions named. This file exists so the
thread can be picked up later, or cross-pollinated, without re-reading the
paper or the session.

## 0. The one-paragraph version

WILD (Zhao, Chang, Paudel, Park, Liu, Aurelio, Oliva, Fernandez-Ruiz;
Cornell; bioRxiv 2026.08.25.747153, not peer-reviewed; GPL-3 hardware and
firmware at `github.com/ayalab1/Neurologger`) is a <1.5 g head-mounted board
for mice that records 64 neural channels plus an IMU, a camera and an
ultrasonic microphone, and — the part that matters here — runs three
on-board pipelines that turn those streams into closed-loop stimulation
without a host in the loop. The link to OBC is architectural, not
biological: WILD's loop is *cheap always-on detector → learned vetting stage
invoked only on candidates → act*, on one MCU, with the host reduced to a
clock and a log. OBC had the same two stages inverted in place — the learned
stage (the mushroom body) on the host, the cheap rules on the node — and no
vetting between a candidate and an action at all. Step 1 gave the node three
of WILD's four ingredients (a detector on the right input, a persistence
vet, a history-relative threshold) and the log that the fourth (a learned
vet) will train on. Nothing learned runs on a node yet, on purpose.

## 1. What WILD is (from the paper; every number is theirs)

**Hardware.** One MCU (the stimulation module is an STM32G071; the main
MCU's part number is not given in the text, only "compact high-performance
microcontroller unit") does acquisition, microSD storage, processing and
BLE, with no external memory or co-processor — that is the weight and power
win. 64-channel amplifier at 20 kHz; 23.3 × 15.7 mm; <1.5 g bare, ~4.5 g
with case and battery; ~3 h of 64 ch/20 kHz on a 130 mAh cell, ~9 h on a
rat-sized ~5 g cell; idle ~1 mW, ~300 h on 80 mAh; power mode switched by
BLE command or by an on-board event (IMU says the animal moved). Parylene-C
flexible probes bonded directly to the board pads (no connector; tissue-
compliant; single units stable across a week outdoors). 9-axis IMU; 320×320
camera at 16 Hz on the pupil; ultrasonic mic on the head, which separates
vocaliser from listener by amplitude (969 calls). Stim module: 0.6 g,
STM32G071 + TPS61151 dual current driver, 35 mA/channel, PL450B laser
diodes, four GPIO pins to the main board.

**Time.** BLE carries control and sync, not data (data stays on the card;
only selected LFP channels stream). Up to seven devices live per host, more
by sequential connection. NTP-style two-way timestamp exchange, 20 s on
connect and repeated during recording, to ~1 ms; external events stamped
on the PC clock via a GPIO board and aligned through the calibrated RTC.
They measure application-level BLE latency including scheduler and
connection-interval effects, not radio propagation.

**The three on-board pipelines.**

| Stage | What it does | Latency | Always on? |
|---|---|---|---|
| DSP chain | channel mixer (any linear combination: CSD, PCA/ICA, reference subtraction) → anti-alias 500 Hz → downsample 1250 Hz → first-order bandpass → envelope (rectify+LPF, or Hilbert-transformer pair → phase too) → threshold at k × baseline mean, or set remotely | ~5 µs | yes |
| TinyML vet | on a DSP detection only: CNN (subsample) + GRU over the preceding 0.5 s, trained to reject EMG artefact; ripple-detection AUROC 0.717 → 0.844, paired bootstrap Δ −0.127 (95 % CI −0.162 to −0.092) | <10 ms | no — per candidate |
| IMU motif GRU | 1 s windows of pitch, roll, magnitude, jerk; labels from video (DeepLabCut) with minority oversampling; deeper GRU won; labels logged and sent wirelessly | ~100 ms | yes |

Models are trained on a PC (TensorFlow, grid search), quantised to TFLite,
compiled with STEdgeAI, and baked into the firmware. Nothing learns on the
device. Offline analysis is MATLAB + Python (Kilosort, phy, CellExplorer,
DeepSqueak, UMAP over wavelet features à la Berman 2014).

**What it bought them.** Tethered mice spent most of their social time
pushing conspecifics away; WILD mice matched unimplanted ones on distance
and interaction time. Behaviour-specific CA1–lateral-septum theta coherence;
vocalisations anticorrelated with sharp-wave ripples but recruiting ~40 % of
CA1 cells; 1,636 place cells from nine mice living 15 days in a 38 × 15 m
outdoor enclosure, ripple rate higher at night; closed-loop ripple
interruption that silenced the associated firing; theta-phase-locked
stimulation that broke pyramidal phase locking; the real-time IMU ethogram
tracked the manual one.

**Limits they state.** High-bandwidth recording is hours, not days. No
on-device vision (OpenCV does not fit), so pupil is offline. Models fixed
at compile time. Behaviour-classifier latency ~100 ms, an order of
magnitude above the neural path. Data: Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.18879184
(analysis scripts and processed data; raw binaries on request).

## 2. Why it is the right model for the spinal tier

Three things transfer, none of them the biology.

1. **The split by cost.** A detector that costs microseconds runs on every
   sample; a model that costs milliseconds runs on candidates; the host
   runs on episodes. OBC's tiers are the same shape — reflex rule (1 s tick)
   / [future] node-side vet / mushroom body on the host (per turn) — and the
   paper's discipline is that each stage only ever sees what the cheaper one
   passed. The mushroom body already works this way with respect to the LLM.
2. **Thresholds relative to history, not to the scale.** WILD's ripple
   detector fires at k × the baseline mean. A fixed threshold on a signal
   that drifts with room and load (the XIAO's die temperature; a battery
   under seasonal temperature; RSSI) is either wrong now or wrong later.
3. **The host as clock and log.** WILD's host does not decide; it
   synchronises and stores. OBC's host does decide (posture), but the part
   of it that touches the node — `descend`, confirmed by `cmd_result`,
   recorded on `descending.<node>` — is exactly clock-and-log, and the
   transition log added in step 1 is the other half of that.

What does *not* transfer: the electrodes, the animal, the IACUC, and the
claim that any of this is "brain mapping". The paper's mapping is place
cells and ripples in mice. The fly thread (`CONNECTOME-2026-09.md`) and
this one meet only at the shape of the loop: sensor → sparse label →
descending modulation of a local reflex.

## 4. The six questions, answered 2026-09-13

1. **Sensor on the node?** An IMU on the next node; option 1 written against
   die temperature now so it is measurable this week. (See §6.1 for the trap
   this uncovered.)
2. **Which rule fire is untrusted?** `safe-link-offline` — worst consequence
   (a node concluding it is alone), and a measured failure mode. It turned out
   not to need a vet: it was measuring the wrong link (§5.1).
3. **Where must the decision live?** Node-local only for what must beat the
   LoRa round-trip plus retry; everything else reports and waits. No third
   "fast-learned" tier until option 3 exists — the three-concrete-uses rule.
4. **Cost of a wrong action?** Unvetted candidates may report, never act. The
   LED is the explicit exception because it is free.
5. **Labels from where?** Start the log before the model: every fire carries
   its evidence from the day the rule ships (§5.4). Train nothing until the log
   has hundreds of transitions with hand-assigned ground truth.
6. **Model fixed at flash?** Yes, as WILD; model hash rides in provenance
   beside the firmware version. An on-device learner would break byte-identical
   determinism, and the 228-byte frame has no room for model updates.

## 5. Step 1 — shipped, with what each commit found

### 5.1 `7c6a92c` — `safe-link-offline` measures the link it names

The node's silence clock was reset by USB bytes only; the spine-UART intake
that receives mesh commands never touched it. A mesh-commanded node with USB
closed held "host link lost" for good; edge-triggering (66179d5) had made
that *quiet*, not true. WILD's channel-mixer point: the detector was wired to
one of its two inputs.

The fix moved once on the bench. Placed at `command_targets_us`, the node
**never went offline at all**: the bridge forwards every verified frame to
the node's UART, the base's own 5 s `gw_keepalive` has no `to`, so it
"targets us" — station liveness was being counted as the host. The reset now
sits on a line `handle_request` accepts: a command is host contact, a
keepalive is not. `scripts/bench_link_contact.py --watch-usb` records the
node's own `link_state` beside the mesh's, which is what separated "never
went offline" from "the mesh lost the report" (the mesh did lose one on the
baseline run). PASS: offline at 30.9 s on both sides, online after the first
mesh command, no offline across 60 s of commands
(`results/bench_link_contact-20260913-114342.json`; walkthrough §A5i).

Cost the truthful rule carries: the host sends no keepalives to nodes, so a
quiet host means offline 30 s after its last command, reported on each
transition. If that chatter ever matters, the fix is a host-side keepalive
command, not a longer timeout.

**Live, 2026-09-13 12:52–13:34, read from the running brain's `world.db`.**
Three things the bench could not show:

1. *Posture reached the node.* 12:52:39, objective "In two sentences … how
   does a fruit fly's mushroom body …", novelty **0.372** > 0.25 → cautious →
   `descend` slot 0 = 0.15 → `active [[0,0.15]] applied 1`, first attempt,
   within a second. 13:10:41, a familiar objective (novelty 0.03) cleared it
   (`active []`, 3 attempts — the base had just come back from a bench
   reset; the 13:09 attempt died with `serial I/O thread has exited` and the
   restart retried it). The body → policy → mesh → node → reflex loop, closed
   in the running system for the first time.
2. *The supervisor and the node were flapping each other at 30 s.* The
   deployed `[mesh_supervisor] stale_ms` was 30000 against a 30 s node
   beacon (the firmware's own comment asks for ≥ 3×). Every 2–5 min the
   supervisor declared the node offline ("no mesh message for ~31 s"), sent a
   `capabilities` recovery probe, the probe counted as host contact (the rule
   above doing its job), the node went online, replied, the supervisor said
   healthy — 11 recovery probes and 12 link-state transitions in 17 minutes,
   and one System 2 wake ("a mesh node is presumed lost") at 13:10:51. The
   supervisor's probes were, by accident, the only host keepalive the node
   received. Fixed in the live config: `stale_ms = 90000`, task restarted
   13:31:24; in the 2.5 min after, 6 beacons ingested and no health,
   recovery or link-state entry at all.
3. *What (2) makes concrete.* With the supervisor quiet, the node hears
   nothing from the host between posture changes and reports `host link
   lost` after 30 s — true, and consumed by nobody: the node's escalate
   reports land as facts (`mesh.<node>.reflex`), and the System 2 wake came
   from the supervisor's own escalation. So the choice above is decided for
   now: **"host link" on a mesh node means "commanded recently", and it is
   not read as a fault.** A host keepalive is airtime spent to make a rule
   feel better, until something on the node needs to know.

Also confirmed live: every reflex report since the reflash carries `ev`
(`[30857.0]` on the link rule, `[34.3]` on `die-cool`); `spine.auth.gw-40`
374 accepted, 0 rejected.

### 5.2 `9dc60fe` — `hold_ms`: persistence beside rate and edge

`ReflexRule::hold_ms`, host and node, same JSON, `serde(default)`. The
condition must have held without a break for that long before the rule may
fire; a drop resets it; judged at tick resolution; composes with
`fire_on_change` (one fire per run, once the hold has elapsed; a run shorter
than the hold fires nothing). WILD's duration criterion (a ripple is 20–600 ms
over threshold) and the cheapest vet there is: no model, no window, one
timestamp per rule. Die rules hold 3000 ms because a ~1 °C-quantised reading
at 1 Hz flickers across its threshold a tick at a time. §A5f 12/12; each
transition reported once, **5 s after the descend rather than 3** — the
hold's cost, in the open (`results/bench_die_rule-20260913-115412.json`).

### 5.3 `8e9101a` — `sensor_baseline`: a threshold on the signal's own history

`Condition::SensorBaseline { entity, op, offset, tau_s }`: true when `value
op baseline + offset`, the baseline a time-aware EMA (α = 1 − e^(−dt/τ) from
real elapsed time). Offset, not ratio, because °C is not a ratio scale
(WILD's k × mean is right for a positive power envelope). The first sample is
the baseline; an unseen entity is false; a bare `Condition::eval` (mission
guards) has no history and answers false; baselines belong to the entity
(kept across a rule push, read but not moved by a bench `reflex_tick`).

Known answers, host and firmware, agreeing to 1e-12: a step fires on its
tick; a 0.01 °C/s ramp for 1000 s (five offsets of climb) never fires — the
EMA lags a ramp by s·(1−α)/α ≈ s·τ; 1 Hz and 0.5 Hz engines hold the same
baseline. One correction to the first draft of those tests, worth keeping: a
sample stands for the interval that *ends* at it, so a reading after a 10 s
gap absorbs 63 % of a step — **a gap in the evidence is not a step in the
signal**, and two cadences agree exactly only when they share the sample the
step is dated to.

`scripts/bench_baseline_rule.py` pushes `die-rising` (die > baseline(60 s)
+ 2 °C, hold 3 s). Steady phase PASS (90 s at 34.3 °C, no fire;
`results/bench_baseline_rule-20260913-120857.json`). **The `--warm` phase —
a thumb on the XIAO's metal can for ~20 s — has not been run and is the one
that proves the rule acts.** Walkthrough §A5j.

### 5.4 `8ae861f` — every reflex report carries the evidence it fired on

`ev`: one number per entity the rule's condition reads, depth-first, first
occurrence only, `null` where the snapshot lacked one. `bl`: the baseline
behind each `sensor_baseline` leaf. Arrays by position, not maps by name —
the report rides a 228-byte line, an entity name is ~24 bytes, and the rule
id names the rule the host holds. Three decimals on the wire (a reading
widened from f32 printed as `34.29999923706055` on the first run; 1e-3 is
below every sensor quantum here). `error` only when there is one:
`"error":null` was 13 bytes of every report, and with the evidence aboard
the escalate shape had 7 bytes left. `tests/spine_payload_budget.rs`
measures the three report shapes; the escalate report with one `ev` value is
the tightest mesh payload at **208 B, 20 spare**, and the gate names it.
The host stores the report whole as `mesh.<node>.reflex`; nothing there
changed. Walkthrough §A5k.

**Pointers.** Host engine `crates/obc-reflex/src/lib.rs` (`Baseline`,
`hold_ms`, `eval_with`); node mirror `firmware/obc-esp32-s3/src/reflex.rs`
(`FiredReflex::from_rule`, `collect_entities_ordered` = the `ev` contract);
`firmware/obc-esp32-s3/src/main.rs` (link contact, report shape);
`config.example.toml` die rules; CHANGELOG entries of 2026-09-13.

## 6. Step 2 — the IMU motif classifier as a slot value (planned)

**Goal.** A node reports a *behaviour*, not a sample: a sensor slot whose
level is "motif X with confidence c", so `descend` gates on motifs rather
than raw readings. This is the most fly-shaped reading of WILD: WILD's IMU
GRU produces the sparse label the descending path acts on, which is the
mushroom body's job at the node's timescale, and it matches the Shiu readout
in `experiments/lif-fly/RESULTS.md` (channel = behaviour module).

**Preconditions, in order.**

### 6.1 Make the stubs absent — found while filing this

`firmware/obc-esp32-s3/src/main.rs`: `read_sensor_snapshot` reads
`sensor.accel_x/y/z` from `mpu6050`, and `sensor_read_stub` answers
`0.01 / 0.00 / 9.81` when no I2C bus is present (also `bme280` 22.5 / 55.0 /
1013.25 and `sht31`). The bench XIAO has no I2C bus, so **every reflex
snapshot today carries a constant, plausible-looking 9.81 m/s² in
`sensor.accel_z`** — and `sensor.temperature` is the 22.5 stub until the
DHT22 overrides it. `max17048` is deliberately absent from the stub so safing
stays dormant; the accelerometer and environment stubs were not given the
same care. A `sensor_baseline` rule on `accel_z` would run for weeks on a
constant. The `ev` log makes this visible (a constant 9.81 in every report),
which is the log doing its job, but the first commit of step 2 is: stubs do
not enter the reflex snapshot; `sensor_read` may still answer with a marked
stub for bring-up. Same rule as `max17048`. This is the platform's
never-invent-physical-data invariant, in firmware.

### 6.2 The IMU

The BOM already lists an MPU6050 at 0x68 on the I2C bus (SDA 5, SCL 6 on
the XIAO map; 15/7 on the Waveshare, which has a QMI8658 at 0x6B) and a
driver exists (`sensors::SensorBus`). Wire one; confirm real readings replace
the stub; record what the node reads at rest (bias, noise at 1 Hz) as a
result file before writing any rule.

### 6.3 Features and windows on the node

WILD's features: pitch, roll from the accelerometer (they used an AHRS
filter offline; on-node a complementary filter is enough), signal magnitude,
jerk, over 1 s windows. Decide the node tick for the IMU (1 Hz is far too
slow for motion; WILD's IMU sampled at hundreds of Hz into 1 s windows).
This is the first place the reflex tick's 1 s cadence stops being adequate,
and the first place a second, faster loop on the node earns its place: the
three-concrete-uses rule says name the three before adding it.

### 6.4 Labels

WILD labelled video by hand (two experts, cross-checked) and oversampled
rare classes. OBC has no video of a node moving and no motion yet. The
labelled log must come from recorded windows with ground truth assigned by
hand — which is why §5.4 shipped before any of this. Do not train on
anything until that log exists and is committed as a result.

### 6.5 The train → flash path

WILD: TensorFlow → TFLite post-training quantisation → STEdgeAI → firmware.
OBC's node is Rust on esp-idf; the equivalents are TFLite-Micro or ESP-NN
reached through C FFI, or a hand-written GRU in Rust if the model is small
enough to be transcribed (a few hundred weights would be). Model hash in
provenance beside the firmware version. The `DECISIONS.md` entry *No
language model on a node* (OBC-Prime, 2026-09-13) already draws the line: a
node-side policy is a few-dozen-weight sensor→slot map, not an LM.

## 7. Step 3 — the learned vet, and the data option (both waiting)

**The ML vet** is WILD's second pipeline: on a candidate from a cheap rule,
run a small model over the preceding window and answer real / artefact
before acting. It needs (a) a signal with structure in the window — the die
temperature has none; the IMU will — and (b) the labelled log of §6.4. Its
non-ML form (`hold_ms`, plus cross-slot agreement through the mixer idea if
a second sensor arrives) is already the vet in use. Target for the ML form,
once there is data: whatever rule the `ev` log shows firing falsely most.

## 8. Refused, and why (this thread's additions to the standing list)

| Refused | Why |
|---|---|
| Counting a station keepalive as host contact | Station liveness is not the host; it made the node incapable of going offline |
| A longer link timeout to hide the truthful rule's chatter | The chatter is the rule being right; a host keepalive is the honest fix |
| Evidence as `{entity: value}` maps in reports | ~24 bytes per name on a 228-byte line; the rule id already names the entities |
| Full-precision floats on the wire | 13 bytes of digits the sensor never had |
| A "fast-learned" tier before the IMU exists | Nothing on the node has structure a model would improve; architecture for its own sake |
| A stub value in a reflex snapshot (§6.1, to be enforced) | A plausible number a rule can act on for weeks |
| An on-device learner | Breaks byte-identical determinism; no frame room for model updates |

## 8a. Added the same evening: the node comes back whole

Two commits after the filing, both forced by the live log (walkthrough
§A5l, §A5m; OBC-Prime `docs/DECISIONS.md` 2026-09-13 "Rules survive a
node's reboot"). The host re-pushes a node's limits whenever it names a boot
the host has not pushed against — announcement, beacon (which now says
`deny-all` until told), or any reply — and retries while the beacon still
says so. Rules persist on the node in NVS, tagged and validated, because
they carry no authority and cannot cross the mesh. And the base station,
it turned out, could not carry a `set_limits` at all: its console read
from a 128-byte FIFO, every `set_limits` ever sent over the mesh died there,
and the census's 228-byte budget had never been exercised on the air
(`scripts/probe_mesh_frame_size.py`). Fixed at the station. A node reset
now ends with the node whole in about a minute with nobody touching it —
the precondition every step below assumed.
